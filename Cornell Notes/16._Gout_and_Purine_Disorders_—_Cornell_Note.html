LO 1.01 • Purine Flux

Purine Pool & Xanthine Oxidase

Purine Nucleotide Breakdown & Terminal Oxidation

LO 1.01 • Slides 4–5
Nucleotide-to-Base Cascade
  • Pool Inputs & Salvage Dominance: Diet, de novo synthesis, and cell turnover feed the nucleotide pool. 90% of our needs come from salvage, whereas only 10% of our needs come from de novo synthesis.
  • Two Metabolic Fates: Nucleotides (AMP, GMP, IMP) are stripped to nucleosides and purine bases, which face strictly two fates: salvage back into nucleotides or irreversible degradation to urate.
Xanthine Oxidase & Clearance
  • Xanthine Convergence: AMP, GMP, and IMP are funneled into xanthine, which xanthine oxidase oxidizes directly into uric acid (the final human catabolic product).
  • Dual Excretion Split: Once filtered by the kidney, plasma urate is eliminated predominantly via the urine (2/3) with the remaining fraction cleared through the intestines (1/3).
Lecturer High-Yield Focus (Dr. Gardner)

"There are only two fates for purine bases: salvage or degradation to urate. Normally, 90% of our needs are met by salvage and only 10% by de novo synthesis."

Second-Order Board Clue

If an exam vignette describes impaired renal clearance of purine breakdown products, recall that two-thirds of urate exits in the urine after xanthine oxidase generates uric acid from xanthine.

LO 1.02 • Evolutionary Genetics

Urate Oxidase & Recombinant Biologics

NON-PRIMATE PRODUCT Allantoin (water-soluble)
HUMAN GENE LOCUS 1p22 (non-functional)
TLS BIOLOGIC Rasburicase
REFRACTORY GOUT BIOLOGIC Pegloticase

Loss of Human Urate Oxidase & Therapeutic Replacement

LO 1.02 • Slide 9
Evolutionary Block at 1p22
  • Allantoin Conversion: Many non-primate species express urate oxidase, converting poorly soluble uric acid into the far more water-soluble allantoin.
  • Human 1p22 Locus: Humans and other primates possess the urate oxidase gene (1p22), but lack functional enzyme activity and therefore stop purine catabolism at uric acid.
Recombinant Urate Oxidase Agents
  • Acute Oncology Use: Rasburicase is a recombinant urate oxidase enzyme approved specifically for the treatment of tumor lysis syndrome.
  • Refractory Chronic Gout: Pegloticase is approved for severe, treatment-refractory chronic gout, enzymatically clearing accumulated urate to allantoin.
Lecturer High-Yield Focus (Dr. Gardner)

"Humans have the urate oxidase gene at 1p22, yet we lack the enzyme and stop at uric acid—making recombinant urate oxidase (Rasburicase and Pegloticase) directly actionable therapies."

Second-Order Board Clue

Match Rasburicase to acute chemotherapy-induced tumor lysis syndrome and Pegloticase to severe, treatment-refractory chronic gout; both convert uric acid to allantoin.

Chemotherapy-Induced Neoplastic Lysis & Metabolic Tetrad

LO 2.01 • Slide 10
Intracellular Release Mechanism
  • Rapid Proliferating Cell Death: Chemotherapy destroys a massive burden of rapidly proliferating neoplastic cells, most commonly in leukemias (3–7% incidence) and lymphomas (4–11% incidence).
  • Electrolyte & Purine Spill: Intracellular nucleic acids and ions flood the bloodstream, producing hyperuricemia (from purine catabolism), hyperkalemia, and hyperphosphatemia.
Hypocalcemia & Renal Collapse
  • Calcium-Phosphate Complexing: Excess released phosphate binds serum calcium, driving secondary hypocalcemia and completing the life-threatening TLS tetrad.
  • Renal & Acid-Base Sequelae: Massive urate filtration and tubular precipitation frequently culminate in acidosis and acute renal failure.
Lecturer High-Yield Focus (Dr. Gardner)

"Tumor lysis syndrome is a very serious, potentially life-threatening response to chemotherapy—watch for hyperuricemia, hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, and hypocalcemia alongside acidosis and renal failure."

Second-Order Board Clue

A leukemia or lymphoma patient developing oliguria and hypocalcemia shortly after starting chemotherapy has tumor lysis syndrome; Rasburicase rapidly degrades the offending uric acid to allantoin.

LO 2.02 • Urate Nephropathy

Acute Urate Precipitation & Clearance

ACUTE TLS BIOLOGIC Rasburicase

Renal Urate Precipitation vs. Chronic Gouty Joint Deposition

LO 2.02 • Slides 9–10, 13, 16
Acute Tubular & Tract Risk
  • Extra-Articular Precipitation: Hyperuricemia does not only threaten joints; monosodium urate precipitates in the kidney and bladder, forming MSU stones (nephrolithiasis/urolithiasis).
  • Acute vs. Chronic Hyperuricemia: Acute hyperuricemia (such as in TLS) does not typically cause acute gouty arthritis—gout develops after prolonged/chronic hyperuricemia, whereas acute spikes threaten renal failure.
Enzymatic Rescue with Rasburicase
  • Immediate Solubilization: Because humans lack urate oxidase, administering recombinant Rasburicase converts insoluble uric acid into water-soluble allantoin.
  • Rapid Renal Unloading: Converting urate to allantoin directly prevents further tubular crystal deposition and relieves acute urate burden.
Lecturer High-Yield Focus (Dr. Gardner)

"True or False: Acute hyperuricemia is the primary cause of most cases of gout? False—gout requires years of hyperuricemia, whereas acute hyperuricemia in TLS threatens renal failure and stones."

Second-Order Board Clue

When asked why Rasburicase is preferred in acute tumor lysis syndrome, identify its mechanism as urate oxidase conversion of uric acid into highly water-soluble allantoin.

Adenosine Deaminase Blockade & Selective Lymphocyte Toxicity

LO 3.01 • Slides 6–7
Dual Deamination Reactions
  • Core Purine Catabolism Role: Adenosine deaminase (ADA) catalyzes two critical deamination reactions in purine degradation: adenosine to inosine and deoxyadenosine to deoxyinosine.
  • Upstream Metabolite Buildup: Loss of ADA causes accumulation of adenosine (plus AMP) and deoxyadenosine (plus dAMP).
Why Lymphocytes Are Hit Hardest
  • Tissue Specificity: Lymphocytes possess the highest ADA enzymatic activity in the human body, making lymphocyte progenitors uniquely vulnerable.
  • Phosphorylation Trap: Excess accumulated deoxyadenosine is phosphorylated into excess dATP, which builds up selectively inside lymphocytes.
Lecturer High-Yield Focus (Dr. Gardner)

"Lymphocytes have the highest activity of ADA in the body. When ADA is deficient, excess deoxyadenosine gets converted into excess dATP, especially in lymphocytes."

Second-Order Board Clue

If asked which purine degradation step fails in ADA deficiency, pinpoint the conversion of adenosine to inosine and deoxyadenosine to deoxyinosine, trapping dATP inside developing lymphocytes.

LO 3.02 • SCID Mechanism

Ribonucleotide Reductase Arrest & SCID

Allosteric Inhibition of DNA Replication & SCID Presentation

LO 3.02 • Slide 8 & FQ 3
Ribonucleotide Reductase Block
  • Normal dNTP Generation: Deoxynucleoside triphosphates (dNTPs) required for DNA replication are generated from NTPs by ribonucleotide reductase.
  • Feedback Inhibition by dATP: Excess dATP potently inhibits ribonucleotide reductase, starving lymphocytes of dGTP, dCTP, and dTTP.
Progenitor Failure & FQ 3 Case
  • Arrested Proliferation: Without balanced dNTPs, DNA replication fails in lymphocyte progenitors, eliminating lymphocyte production and causing severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID).
  • Formative Quiz Case (FQ 3): A 2-year-old girl evaluated for SCID due to ADA deficiency demonstrates a buildup of dATP in lymphocytes inhibiting ribonucleotide reductase.
Lecturer High-Yield Focus (Dr. Gardner)

"Lymphocytes end up with excess dATP but minimal amounts of dGTP, dCTP, and dTTP—causing a lack of DNA replication in lymphocyte progenitors and severe combined immunodeficiency."

Second-Order Board Clue

Board distractor trap: dATP does not directly inhibit DNA polymerase in ADA-SCID; it inhibits ribonucleotide reductase, starving the cell of dGTP, dCTP, and dTTP.

Common-Ion Sodium Effect & Plasma Urate Saturation

LO 4.01 • Slides 15–18
Why Plasma Solubility Plummets
  • Aqueous vs. Plasma Contrast: In pure water without sodium, monosodium urate (MSU) has a high saturation limit of 110 mg/dL.
  • Ksp Equilibrium Rule: MSU solubility depends on both sodium and urate: Ksp = [Na+][Urate] = 4.0 × 10⁻⁵. Exceeding this product forces MSU to precipitate out of plasma.
140 mM Sodium Suppression
  • Physiologic Sodium Load: Because plasma maintains a sodium concentration of ~140 mM, the saturation point of urate drops drastically from 110 mg/dL down to 7.0 mg/dL at 37°C.
  • Chronicity Requirement: Sustained plasma urate above 7.0 mg/dL over years drives crystal nucleation in joints, kidneys, and bladder.
Lecturer High-Yield Focus (Dr. Gardner)

"In water with no Na+, the saturation point is 110 mg/dL. Because plasma has ~140 mM Na+ and Ksp = [Na+][Urate] = 4.0 × 10⁻⁵, plasma urate solubility at 37°C is only 7.0 mg/dL."

Second-Order Board Clue

Any increase in local sodium or decrease in plasma saturation lowers the threshold for monosodium urate precipitation once 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ is exceeded.

LO 4.02 • Clinical Presentation

Temperature Rule & Polarized Microscopy

TEMPERATURE RELATIONSHIP Lower temperature → lower MSU solubility
CLASSIC FIRST JOINT Great toe metatarsophalangeal joint
MICROSCOPY HALLMARK Negative birefringent crystals

Distal Cooling Predisposition & Phagocytic Joint Inflammation

LO 4.02 • Slides 14, 17–19 & FQ 1
Why the Great Toe Hurts First
  • Salt Solubility Physics: The lower the temperature, the lower the solubility of a salt in solution. Core temperature under the tongue is ~37°C, whereas peripheral joints (knee, ankle, and especially the great toe) are substantially cooler.
  • Demographic & Seasonal Risk: Peripheral temperatures also tend to be lower in older individuals and during the winter, further promoting crystal precipitation.
Synovial Inflammation & FQ 1 Case
  • Microscopy & Phagocytosis: Joint aspirate shows monosodium urate crystals exhibiting negative birefringence under polarized light microscopy; neutrophils and macrophages attempt to phagocytize these crystals, triggering painful inflammation.
  • Formative Quiz Case (FQ 1): A 66-year-old man with 10/10 pain and a swollen right great toe metatarsophalangeal joint illustrates: area of lower temperature → lower MSU solubility.
Lecturer High-Yield Focus (Dr. Gardner)

"Compare under the tongue (~37°C) to the knee, ankle, and great toe—where does a person typically first experience painful gout? Lower temperature lowers salt solubility."

Second-Order Board Clue

Pair an acutely swollen first metatarsophalangeal joint with negative birefringence under polarized microscopy and neutrophil/macrophage engulfment of monosodium urate crystals.

Underexcretion Dominance & Proximal Tubular URAT1 Exchange

LO 5.01 • Slides 21–22
Etiologic Split (~90% vs. ~10%)
  • Underexcretion Predominates: Hyperuricemia is classified into overproduction versus decreased excretion, with ~90% of gout cases resulting from decreased renal excretion.
  • Organic Acidosis Link: Both lactic acidosis and ketoacidosis elevate systemic organic anions that directly impair renal urate clearance.
Tubular Anion Loading Mechanism
  • URAT1 Counter-Transport: Lactate and ketones (3-hydroxybutyrate and acetoacetate) load proximal tubular cells with anions that serve as substrates for URAT1 and other transporters.
  • Exchange Consequence: These intracellular anions are exchanged outward into the tubular lumen in return for luminal urate, causing enhanced reabsorption of urate and reduced urate excretion alongside greater excretion of lactate/ketones.
Lecturer High-Yield Focus (Dr. Gardner)

"Both lactate and ketones (3-hydroxybutyrate, acetoacetate) load tubular cells and become substrate for URAT1—exchanged for urate in the proximal tubule, enhancing urate reabsorption."

Second-Order Board Clue

When a patient with ketoacidosis or lactic acidosis develops hyperuricemia, identify URAT1 antiport exchange of 3-hydroxybutyrate, acetoacetate, or lactate for urate in the proximal tubule.

Dual-Hit Mechanism of Beer, Seafood & Ethanol Metabolism

LO 5.02 • Slides 23–24 & FQ 5
Dietary Purines: Beer vs. Wine
  • Fermentation Purine Load: Alcohol is a primary dietary contributor to gout, with beer consumption most strongly linked because fermentation produces an especially high purine content (amplified in unfiltered beer and seafood).
  • Beverage Contrast: Unlike beer, moderate wine consumption does not appear to carry the same elevated gout risk.
NADH Shift & FQ 5 Case
  • Dual Hepatic & Renal Mechanisms: Ethanol metabolism generates abundant NADH, which shifts glucose metabolism from pyruvate to lactate (oxidizing NADH back to NAD+) so lactate drives renal urate reabsorption via URAT1. Meanwhile, hepatic AMP/adenosine rises, increasing urate production.
  • Formative Quiz Case (FQ 5): A 64-year-old woman with painful left great toe who eats seafood and drinks homemade unfiltered beer (urine pH 4.5) has impaired renal urate excretion driven by increased NADH.
Lecturer High-Yield Focus (Dr. Gardner)

"Ethanol metabolism generates large amounts of NADH, shifting pyruvate to lactate—and that lactate stimulates urate resorption at the kidney, while high adenosine increases urate production."

Second-Order Board Clue

If a question asks which cofactor directly drives the metabolic block on renal urate excretion after drinking unfiltered beer, select NADH (which drives pyruvate to lactate conversion).

Purine Base Salvage Mechanics & PRPP Ribose Donation

LO 6.01 • Slide 4 & FQ 2
Salvage Enzyme Reactions
  • Dual Salvage Enzymes: Purine base salvage relies on two enzymes: HGPRT (hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase) and APRT.
  • HGPRT Products: Using hypoxanthine or guanine as the purine base substrate, HGPRT synthesizes IMP or GMP in a single step, accounting for ~90% of purine needs normally.
PRPP Donor & FQ 2 Case
  • Ribose Sugar Source: PRPP (5-phosphoribosyl-1-pyrophosphate) provides the activated ribose-phosphate sugar required for HGPRT to convert free purine bases back into mononucleotides.
  • Formative Quiz Case (FQ 2): When asked which molecule provides the ribose sugar for synthesizing IMP from hypoxanthine (or from guanine), the correct substrate is PRPP.
Lecturer High-Yield Focus (Dr. Gardner)

"HGPRT salvages hypoxanthine or guanine using PRPP as the provider of the ribose sugar—consuming PRPP while producing IMP and GMP."

Second-Order Board Clue

Do not confuse the enzyme (HGPRT) with the ribose sugar donor (PRPP) when synthesizing IMP from hypoxanthine.

LO 6.02 • Lesch-Nyhan

Lesch-Nyhan Overproduction Loop

DEFICIENT ENZYME HGPRT deficiency
HYPERURICEMIA BASIS Overproduction of urate
ACCUMULATED ACTIVATOR Elevated PRPP
LOST FEEDBACK INHIBITORS Low AMP and GMP

Why HGPRT Deficiency Superinduces De Novo Purine Synthesis

LO 6.02 • Slides 4, 21 & FQ 4
Overproduction Mechanism
  • Overproduction vs. Underexcretion: Unlike ~90% of gout cases (underexcretion), Lesch-Nyhan syndrome causes hyperuricemia via massive overproduction of urate.
  • Unconsumed PRPP Surge: Because HGPRT cannot salvage hypoxanthine or guanine, PRPP accumulates instead of being consumed—driving de novo purine synthesis forward.
Lost Feedback & FQ 4 Rescue
  • Dual Regulatory Failure: Failure of salvage leaves PRPP elevated without a corresponding rise in the negative feedback inhibitors AMP and GMP, causing superinduction of de novo synthesis and degradation to urate (1–2% salvage drop).
  • Formative Quiz Case (FQ 4): An elevated level of GMP (guanosine monophosphate) or AMP would inhibit this superinduced step and prevent overproduction in an HGPRT-deficient patient.
Lecturer High-Yield Focus (Dr. Gardner)

"Which is Lesch-Nyhan syndrome—overproduction or underexcretion? Overproduction! Inability to salvage raises PRPP without raising the inhibitors AMP and GMP."

Second-Order Board Clue

If an exam asks what prevents superinduction of the first de novo step in HGPRT deficiency, select GMP (or AMP) acting as allosteric feedback inhibitor against excess PRPP.
